# 10. Quality guidelines

## What this topic covers

Revibe's quality guidelines for phones, tablets, watches and laptops. The guidelines are split into three grades (Excellent, Very good, Good) and set the rules for hardware, screen, body, battery, parts, functions, screen acceptance, banned elements, and the claims limit with its penalty (10.0 [00:01-00:13], 10.0 [01:01], 10.0 [03:16-03:38], 10.0 [05:01]). The trainer says the guidelines are written so sellers can follow them and avoid as many claims as possible (10.0 [00:07-00:13]).

## How it works

1. Grades and baseline. Devices are rated in one of three grades. Every device must be 100% functional, like a new one, in every grade. The difference from a new device may only be in small details such as battery health or physical condition, never in features (10.0 [00:15], 10.0 [00:20], 10.0 [00:29-00:35]).
2. Screen and body. Excellent and Very good must show no sign of use on the screen. Excellent body must look like a new device. Very good and Good may show light marks (10.0 [00:37], 10.0 [00:48-00:54]).
3. Battery. Minimum battery health is 95% (Excellent), 90% (Very good), 85% (Good). Battery is checked with 3utool (10.0 [01:01], 10.0 [01:07]).
4. Camera dots. Check with the camera in portrait mode against white paper or a white wall. Limits are per grade (10.0 [01:12-01:18]). The limits conflict between two rows of the deck; see Contradictions.
5. Plug and parts. The plug or socket type must match the country's specs, including for laptops (10.0 [01:24-01:27]). Display: Excellent needs an original or DD display; Very good and Good accept a high-quality copy (10.0 [01:30-01:38]). Glass, cover, buttons and others may be high-quality copies, but the device must never show a non-original parts message. On Samsung, the S Pen must be original (10.0 [01:43-01:55]).
6. Functions. Functions are the same in every grade. The only grade difference in the functions section is the camera dots (10.0 [02:00-02:13]). Phone functions include data deletion, chargers and cables, unlocking, IMEI, phone carrier and temperature (10.0 [01:59]).
7. Screen acceptance (page 2). Original and DD screens are accepted. A GW Good quality replacement is accepted only on Good. A GW & others Bad quality replacement is not accepted in any grade. A good-quality screen has normal size, is thin, has a smooth touch, whitish backlight and normal sensors, and does not overheat. Thick screens and screens with a pinkish or yellowish tint are refused (10.0 [02:15-03:05]).
8. Other issues. Check for gaps around the display and an Apple logo that stays frozen on the screen (10.0 [03:09]).
9. Banned elements (page 3). Banned elements are not accepted in any grade or on any device and should never lead to a claim. If the supplier does one, "the supplier clearly didn't follow the quality guidelines" (10.0 [03:16-03:22]). The list is grouped as Grading, Wrong item, Parts, and Locks & IMEI (10.0 [03:52-04:46]).
10. Claims limit and penalty. The total of claims should never exceed 2. Above that, a trigger is activated (10.0 [03:27]). AI analyses customer claim forms and flags issues. The KAM checks flagged claims one by one (narration) or the Revibe team checks them (slide). If a claim is confirmed, a 10% penalty applies to each claim received, based on the seller's price (10.0 [03:35-03:52]).
11. Grade pictures. Excellent must look like new. Very good may show a few very small scratches. Good may show a few more (10.0 [04:53-04:56]).
12. Laptops. Laptops follow the same functional rules, listed by category in the functions table. The laptop screen allows only 1 or 2 very small scratches (10.0 [05:01-05:03]).
13. Most common rejection reasons. Battery level, physical condition, camera dots and display issues drive most claims, according to the trainer (10.0 [05:10-05:17]).
14. Recap. "Quality first, fewer claims." Max 2 claims, then a 10% penalty per claim (10.0 [05:22]).

## Contradictions

- Camera dots, Excellent: the Camera dot row and narration say none allowed (10.0 [01:18]), but the Cameras row in the functions deep dive says max 1 small camera dot (10.0 [02:10]). Confirm before quizzing.
- Camera dots, Very good and Good: the Camera dot row says 1 (Very good) and 2 (Good), but the Cameras row puts Very good and Good in one cell as max 2 (10.0 [02:10]). The page flags this as unresolved.
- Who checks claims: the narration says "You, as a KM" check claims one by one (10.0 [03:35]). The slide says the Revibe team checks them (10.0 [03:27]). The caption "AI flags them, you confirm" (10.0 [03:45]) suggests the KAM confirms. Unresolved. "KM" is not on screen.
- Good screen scratches: narration says allowed "as long as you can't see them when the screen is on" (10.0 [00:42]); on-screen wording is that they may be visible but do not interfere when switched on. Confirm the official wording before quoting.
- Replacement screen label: narration says the screen "looks light gray when off" (10.0 [02:33]); on screen that row is labelled "Keyboard".
- Phone carrier: "Must be unlocked to a phone carrier" (10.0 [02:00]) is ambiguous compared with the banned SIM-lock rule (10.0 [04:46]).
- Possibly out of date: "We're now introducing banned elements" (10.0 [03:16]) and "Under the new policy" (10.0 [03:27]) have no effective date. The policy may have changed since the video was made.
- Claims limit scope: the 2-claim limit is not stated as per seller, per period or per order, and the trigger's effect beyond the review step is not explained (10.0 [03:27]).
- Rejection percentages (10.0 [05:12]) have no visible date, period or base, so they cannot be read as a rate of anything.

No other conflicts found within the item.

## Quiz blueprint

Page: 10.0 Quality guidelines (Seller & KAM), 325 s (about 5.4 minutes). Suggested questions: 5 (about one per minute, at most 20).

- Areas to cover: grades and the 100% functional baseline; battery minimums and camera dots; plug and parts rules; screen acceptance; banned elements; claims limit and 10% penalty; laptops; rejection reasons.
- Areas suited to multiple-answer questions: banned-element groups (choose every banned case); screen acceptance by grade (choose every accepted screen type for a given grade); parts allowed as copies per grade; laptop rules.
- Avoid quizzing until confirmed: the camera-dot limit for Excellent and Very good, and who checks claims.

Best scenario seeds:
1. A phone is sold as "Excellent" with visible scratches. Answer: it is a banned element, not accepted in any grade (10.0 [03:58], [03:16]).
2. A phone has 84% battery health and is sold as Good. Answer: below the Good minimum of 85%, so it is a banned element (10.0 [01:01], [03:52]).
3. A Very good device has a GW Good quality replacement screen. Answer: not accepted on Very good; accepted only on Good (10.0 [02:20], [02:16]).
